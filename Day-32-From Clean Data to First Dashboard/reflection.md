## Day 32/120 – From Clean Data to First Dashboard

![](https://github.com/omolola-A/120-days-data-analytics-journey/blob/main/Day-32.png)

Today I moved the cleaned dataset into Power BI and started building the Sales Overview dashboard.

The process was not as smooth as I expected. After importing the data, I had to go back and fix a few data types and carefully set up relationships between tables. A few wrong joins at the start gave me numbers that didn’t make sense, so I had to retrace my steps and align everything properly.

Once that was sorted, I built the first version of the dashboard.

A few things stood out immediately:

* Sales came in at 22.2M, beating the 21.1M target by about 1.1M

* The business is heavily dependent on one category—Bikes drive over 95% of total revenue

* Sales were flat early in the year, then picked up strongly from July and peaked in December

* London and Paris consistently show up as the strongest markets

Looking at the data across years added more context. 2019 looks like a baseline year, 2020 shows growth and some diversification, and 2021 is where revenue really scales—but mostly driven again by the core bike category.

It’s interesting to see growth on paper, but also a clear dependency risk.

Next step is to go beyond the overview and start breaking down where the real opportunities (or weaknesses) are hiding.
